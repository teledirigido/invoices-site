If you invoice a company in Germany, France or the Netherlands without VAT, or you buy services from a business in another EU country, sooner or later Modelo 349 shows up. It's one of the forms that raises the most questions, mainly because you don't pay anything with it: it's purely informative.

Modelo 349 is the **recapitulative return of intra-Community transactions** (declaración recapitulativa de operaciones intracomunitarias). It's where you report, customer by customer and supplier by supplier, the sales of goods and services and the purchases you've made with businesses in other EU countries.

- It's an informative return.
- **It's filed by** any professional with intra-EU transactions, whatever their VAT regime.
- It's filed quarterly if you stay under 50,000 €, monthly if you go over.
- You don't file it if you've had no intra-EU transactions.

::

## What it's actually for

In transactions between businesses in different EU countries, VAT isn't charged by whoever issues the invoice: it's self-assessed by whoever receives it, in their own country. This is what's known as the reverse charge (inversión del sujeto pasivo). You invoice without VAT and your German client declares that VAT in Germany.

The obvious problem is that this way no tax authority sees the full transaction. If you invoice without VAT and nobody checks that your client declared it, the VAT gets lost along the way.

Modelo 349 is the piece that closes that loop. You declare that you sold 8,000 € to a client with a German VAT number, and the Spanish tax agency cross-checks that figure against what that client declared in Germany, through the VIES system. If the numbers don't match, it raises a flag.

## Before Modelo 349: being on the ROI

Modelo 349 only makes sense if you're registered on the **Registro de Operadores Intracomunitarios (ROI)**, the register of intra-Community operators. That's the prior step, and you apply for it with Modelo 036, ticking box 582 and giving the expected date of your first transaction in box 584.

Once you're on the ROI, you get a NIF-IVA (your Spanish tax number with the ES prefix in front) and you appear in the VIES register, where your European clients can verify that you're a valid operator. Without that, you can't invoice EU businesses without VAT: you'd have to charge Spanish VAT just like you would to any domestic client.

It's also worth checking your client's VAT number in VIES before invoicing them without VAT. If the number isn't valid, the exemption doesn't hold and the VAT can be claimed from you.

## Which transactions you report: the keys

Each line of Modelo 349 carries a key (clave) identifying the type of transaction. These are the ones that affect most self-employed people and small businesses:

| Key   | Transaction                                | Typical example                                                                             |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **S** | Intra-Community supplies of services       | You invoice design, development or consulting to a French company.                          |
| **A** | Intra-Community acquisitions of goods      | You buy materials from an Italian supplier and the goods enter Spain.                       |
| **E** | Intra-Community supplies of goods (exempt) | You sell product to a Portuguese company and the goods leave Spain.                         |
| **I** | Intra-Community acquisitions of services   | You buy a service from a business in another EU country and you're the one receiving it.    |
| **T** | Supplies in triangular transactions        | You act as intermediary between a supplier and an end customer, both in other EU countries. |

There are also keys **M** and **H** (supplies following exempt imports, the latter through a fiscal representative) and keys **R**, **D** and **C**, reserved for call-off stock arrangements: transfer of the goods, return to the Member State of origin, and substitution of the intended recipient.

Key **S** is by far the most common among self-employed people working with European clients.

## What doesn't go in Modelo 349

Not everything that crosses a border belongs in Modelo 349. Among the cases left out:

- Transactions with countries **outside the EU**. An invoice to a client in the United States, the United Kingdom or Switzerland is an export, not an intra-Community transaction.
- Sales to **private individuals** in other EU countries. Modelo 349 covers transactions between businesses or professionals identified with a VAT number.
- Supplies where **the recipient has no VAT number** in another Member State.
- Exempt intra-Community acquisitions made as part of a triangular transaction.
- **Intermediation services in property transactions**.

That's the most frequent mistake: putting invoices to private individuals or to clients outside the EU into Modelo 349. Those invoices belong elsewhere, but not here.

## Monthly or quarterly?

This depends only on your volume of transactions, not on your size or your activity.

**You file quarterly** when neither in the quarter you're reporting nor in any of the **four preceding calendar quarters** has the total value of the supplies of goods and services that must be reported in Modelo 349 exceeded **50,000 €** (excluding VAT).

**You file monthly** as soon as that threshold is crossed.

There's a detail that often gets overlooked: if you cross 50,000 € **mid-quarter**, you don't wait for the next quarter to switch. You have to file a return covering the months elapsed since the start of that calendar quarter, within the first twenty calendar days that follow:

- If you cross it in the **first month** of the quarter, you file a monthly return with that month's transactions.
- If you cross it in the **second month**, you file a return covering the first two months of the quarter (what the form marks as a "trimestre truncado", a truncated quarter).

The **annual option no longer exists**. Orden HAC/174/2020 removed it starting with the 2020 returns, so if you were looking for the annual Modelo 349 with its 35,000 € and 15,000 € limits, that route has been closed for years.

## Filing deadlines

| Period                                  | Deadline                                                   |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| January to June, and August to November | The first 20 calendar days of the following month.         |
| July (monthly)                          | During August and the first 20 calendar days of September. |
| December (monthly)                      | The first 30 calendar days of January.                     |
| Q1, Q2 and Q3                           | The first 20 calendar days of April, July and October.     |
| Q4                                      | The first 30 calendar days of January.                     |

Filing is always electronic, through the AEAT's Sede Electrónica: either with the web form or by submitting a file matching the form's record layout.

## What happens if you don't file it

Since no money is attached to Modelo 349, there's no late payment surcharge. The penalty is the one for informative returns: it's calculated per item or set of items omitted or reported incorrectly, with a minimum per return, and it's reduced if you file late but before the tax agency requests it.

The real risk isn't so much the penalty as the mismatch. If your European client declares the transaction and you don't, or the amounts don't agree, the VIES cross-check picks it up and you end up receiving a query. That's why it's worth making sure your Modelo 349 figures match the intra-Community supplies box on your Modelo 303.

## In summary

- Modelo 349 is informative: you detail your intra-EU transactions, but you don't pay anything with it.
- You need to be on the ROI to trade without VAT with EU businesses, and it's worth validating your client's VAT number in VIES.
- The most common keys are **S** (services supplied), **E** (supplies of goods), **A** (acquisitions of goods) and **I** (services received).
- It's quarterly as long as you stay under 50,000 €, and monthly as soon as you go over, switching immediately if it happens mid-quarter.
- If you've had no intra-EU transactions in a period, there's nothing to file.
- Check that your Modelo 349 amounts match your Modelo 303: the VIES cross-check compares both sides of every transaction.
