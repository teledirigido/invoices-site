You notice right after filing: an invoice was missing, an expense you forgot to log in time. It's a common mistake, and in most cases Modelo 130 itself fixes it the following quarter, without you having to cancel or redo anything.

::card

Modelo 130 is cumulative, so the next quarter almost always makes up the difference. What changes the fix is whether the payment has already been charged or not:

- **Not charged yet (direct debit):** you can correct the filing while the direct debit window is still open.
- **Already paid:** you can wait for the next quarter or request a refund through an amendment.
- **Paid too little:** this is fixed with a supplementary filing (declaración complementaria).
::

## Why Modelo 130 can "fix itself"

Each quarter, Modelo 130 calculates 20% of the cumulative net income since January (income minus deductible expenses) and subtracts whatever you already paid in previous quarters of the same year.

The key piece is box 05, which holds the installment payments from previous quarters. If you overpaid in Q3 because some expenses were missing, that amount carries into box 05 of Q4. When the calculation is redone over the full year, Q4 comes out lower, and the total paid for the year ends up correct.

## A round-number example

This is a made-up case to illustrate the mechanics, without withholdings or other boxes. Say you'd already paid 2,500 € between Q1 and Q2.

| Quarter | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Q3 | You declare a cumulative net income of 20,000 € and pay 1,500 € (20% of 20,000 €, minus the 2,500 € already paid). |
| — | You realize you forgot 1,000 € in expenses: the real net income for Q3 was 19,000 €, not 20,000 €. Because of that mistake, you overpaid by 200 €. |
| Q4 | Cumulative net income reaches 25,000 € (now including that 1,000 €). When calculating the Q4 payment, box 05 carries the 1,500 € you already paid in Q3, so the result comes out 200 € lower than it would have without the mistake: you pay 1,000 € instead of 1,200 €. |

The 200 € you overpaid in Q3 is offset by the 200 € you underpay in Q4. The total paid for the year doesn't change, only when you advance it to the tax authority (Hacienda).

## Which path to use, depending on your case

In practice, a Modelo 130 that's already been filed isn't canceled: it's corrected or offset afterward.

| Situation | Path | What to keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You paid too little | Supplementary filing (declaración complementaria) | Same form, checking the supplementary box and referencing the receipt from the original filing. |
| You paid too much and can wait | Offset it in the next quarter | Box 05 of the next quarter includes what you already paid, as in the example. |
| You paid too much and want it back now | Rectification request | As of April 2026, Modelo 130 didn't have the rectifying self-assessment (autoliquidación rectificativa) enabled. Check whether that's still the case. |
| You'd rather redo it entirely | Substitute filing (declaración sustitutiva) | Fully replaces the original. Confirm with the AEAT's online office (Sede Electrónica) that it's available for Modelo 130. |

## If you chose direct debit and haven't been charged yet

This is the case with the most room to maneuver. According to the AEAT's calendar, the direct debit window for each quarter closes a few days before the general filing deadline, and the charge happens on the last day of the filing period.

While the direct debit window is still open, you can change your bank account or cancel the direct debit. Once that window closes, you can't. If you do nothing, the amount will be charged exactly as filed.

Canceling the direct debit doesn't correct the declared amount. For that, you need to file the correction that applies to your case (see the table above), and if you want to keep paying by direct debit, do it before that window closes.

## The condition: the next quarter has to be able to absorb the difference

Waiting for the next quarter only works fully if that quarter comes out with a sufficiently positive amount. If, after subtracting what you already paid, the result is negative, Modelo 130 won't refund you: in the fourth quarter that result is marked as "Negativa" (negative), instead of "A deducir" (to be deducted), which is the option available in the first three quarters.

The excess isn't lost. Modelo 130's installment payments are subtracted from the total due in your income tax return (declaración de la Renta), which is where the difference gets reconciled.

The larger the overpayment in one quarter and the lower the expected income in the next, the more likely it is that the next quarter won't be able to absorb it. Do the math before deciding to wait.

## How to avoid this next quarter

- Log each invoice as you receive it, not on the last day of the deadline.
- Before filing, check that every expense is dated within the quarter.
- If you pay by direct debit, file with margin to spare, not at the last moment.
- In the following quarter, check that box 05 reflects what you actually declared and paid.

## In summary

- Modelo 130 is cumulative: an expense forgotten in one quarter is usually offset automatically in the next, through box 05.
- If you paid too little, the fix is a supplementary filing.
- If you paid by direct debit and that window is still open, you can still correct the filing.
- Waiting for the next quarter only works if that quarter comes out positive; otherwise, the adjustment carries over to your income tax return.
