If you've ever compared the amount on an invoice you issued with what shows up in your AEAT tax data or your income tax draft, it's easy to think something doesn't add up. The explanation is almost always the same: IRPF withholding.

## What IRPF withholding on an invoice is

When a self-employed professional (autónomo) invoices another business or professional, in many cases they must apply an IRPF withholding (usually 15 %, or 7 % during the first three years of activity). The autónomo doesn't collect that withholding: the client withholds it and pays it directly to the tax authority (Hacienda) on the autónomo's behalf, as an advance payment toward their future income tax return.

For example, an invoice with a 1,000 € base amount and a 15 % withholding breaks down like this:

| Item                    | On the invoice                       | At the AEAT                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Taxable base            | 1,000 €                              | 1,000 €                                                     |
| IVA (21 %)              | 210 €                                | —                                                           |
| IRPF withholding (15 %) | −150 €                               | 150 € (advance payment in your favor)                       |
| **Total**               | **1,060 € received in your account** | **1,150 € (1,000 € invoiced + 150 € withholding recorded)** |

The autónomo issues the invoice for 1,000 € plus IVA, but only receives 1,060 € in their account, because the client already paid the 150 € withholding to Hacienda. The AEAT, on the other hand, doesn't see the net amount received: it sees the 1,000 € invoiced and, separately, the 150 € the client declared as withholding under your name.

## Why the AEAT shows a different amount

Here's the key point: the AEAT records the **gross** amount of the invoice (the taxable base, 1,000 € in the example), not the net amount the autónomo actually received in their account. In other words, your tax data or income tax draft will show the 1,000 € invoiced, plus the 150 € withholding the client already paid on your behalf.

This isn't an error or double-counting: the withholding shown by the AEAT is an advance payment that gets deducted later, when calculating the final result of your income tax return. If withholding was applied correctly throughout the year, that amount is subtracted from what you owe (or added to your refund).

## Where to check that everything adds up

- **Modelo 130** (the autónomo's quarterly installment payment): you declare your income and deductible expenses, and can deduct any withholdings applied to you.
- **Modelo 111** (filed by the client): this is where the client declares and pays the withholdings they applied to you.
- **AEAT tax data**: this shows the gross amount invoiced to each client and the total withholdings recorded under your name, based on what your clients declared in their Modelo 111 filings.

If the amount you see at the AEAT doesn't match what you expected, check that the client correctly declared the withholding in their Modelo 111. That's the most common cause of mismatches, not an error in your own bookkeeping.

## In summary

- The AEAT records the gross amount of your invoices, not the net amount you receive in your account.
- IRPF withholding is an advance payment your client pays on your behalf, not an extra expense.
- That withholding is deducted in your income tax return, it isn't lost.
- If the amounts don't match, check that the client correctly declared the withholding in their Modelo 111.
