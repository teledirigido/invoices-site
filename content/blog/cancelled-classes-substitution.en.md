Tom gives private English classes. A student books the four November classes, at 30 € each, and pays for them in advance. Tom issues the invoice. A few days later, the student cancels all four and Tom refunds the money.

It is the same case as Marta's in [A client cancelled a service on an invoice they already paid](/blog/prepaid-classes-cancelled). Marta solved it with a rectifying invoice **by difference**. Tom will use the other way: **by substitution**. Both are correct and lead to the same result.

::card
---

title: Short answer
background: white
shadow: true
---

- The original invoice is not annulled. The student paid, so the invoice was correct.
- Tom issues a **rectifying invoice (factura rectificativa) R1, by substitution (S)**.
- The rectifying invoice shows what the invoice should be after the cancellation. If all the classes are cancelled, **0 €**.
- There is no need to write negative amounts.

::

## What "by substitution" means

A rectifying invoice by substitution takes the place of the original invoice. It does not show the amount to subtract: it shows the amount the invoice should have had.

The reason is **R1**, as in Marta's case. It is the code for an operation that is undone after it was invoiced (art. 80.Dos of the VAT Law). It is not R4, because there was no mistake.

## Which amounts

Private English classes given by an individual are exempt from VAT, because English is a subject in the official curricula. The VAT Law covers it in article 20.Uno.10º:

> Private classes given by individuals on subjects included in the curricula of any of the levels and grades of the education system.

That is why Tom's invoices carry no VAT.

::

The rectifying invoice also shows the **rectification amount**: the difference between what it shows now and what the original invoice showed. The Invoicing Regulation asks for it when the rectifying invoice shows the amounts as they stand after the correction (art. 15.5):

> or as they stand after the correction made, also stating in this case the amount of that correction.

The final result for those classes is 0 €: the student pays nothing and Tom declares no income for them.

## If only part is cancelled

If the student attends two classes and cancels the other two, the rectifying invoice shows what remains: 2 × 30 € = 60 €. The rectification amount is -60 €.

This is how each way works, in both cases:

|                    | Substitution (S)           | Difference (I)         |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| What you write     | What the invoice should be | The amount to subtract |
| All four cancelled | 0 €                        | -120 €                 |
| Two cancelled      | 60 €                       | -60 €                  |

With both types, what remains invoiced is the same: 0 € if the student cancels all four classes and 60 € if they cancel two.

## Which one do I choose?

Both ways are correct. It depends on what you find easier:

- **Substitution (S)** if you prefer to write what the invoice should be. If everything is cancelled, it is the simplest: 0 €.
- **Difference (I)** if you prefer to write only the change. It is the most direct when you know how much you refund.

What you cannot do is mix them. In a rectifying invoice by substitution you do not write the amount to subtract in negative: that is a rectifying invoice by difference.

## In short

- If classes already invoiced are cancelled, the invoice is not annulled: you issue a rectifying invoice **R1**.
- **By substitution (S)**, the rectifying invoice shows what the invoice should be: 0 € if everything is cancelled.
- **By difference (I)**, it shows the amount to subtract, in negative. That is what Marta did in [A client cancelled a service on an invoice they already paid](/blog/prepaid-classes-cancelled).
- Both ways are correct and the result is the same. Choose the one you prefer.
