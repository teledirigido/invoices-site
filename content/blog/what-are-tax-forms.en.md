If you've just registered as self-employed (autónomo) in Spain, you've probably heard about "filing modelo such-and-such" without it being clear what that actually means. Before getting into the details of each one, it's worth understanding what a modelo is and what it's for.

## What a modelo is

A modelo is an official form used by the Spanish tax agency (AEAT) through which a taxpayer declares fiscal information: income, expenses, VAT, withholdings, and so on. Each modelo has a number (130, 303, 111...) that identifies what kind of information it captures and how often it must be filed.

Filing a modelo doesn't always mean paying. Some modelos are self-assessments (you calculate how much you owe or should be refunded), while others are purely informational (summaries the tax agency uses to cross-check data, with no money changing hands).

## Why there are different modelos

Each modelo covers a different aspect of your activity:

- Some declare **taxes you pay yourself** (like the income tax that applies to you as an autónomo).
- Others declare **taxes you manage on behalf of third parties** (like the withholdings you apply to other professionals or employees).
- Others are **annual summaries** of what was already declared quarterly, so the tax agency has a complete picture of the year.

This separation lets the tax agency cross-check information declared by different taxpayers: for example, the withholding you apply to a supplier in your Modelo 111 must match what that supplier declares as withholding received in their Modelo 130.

## Who is required to file them

As an autónomo, you're required to file the modelos that apply to your specific activity. Not every autónomo files the same ones: it depends on whether you charge VAT, whether you're exempt, whether you have employees or subcontract with withholding, whether you deal with EU clients or suppliers, and so on.

That's why the first step when registering is identifying which modelos apply to your case, something that's usually sorted out with your accountant (gestoría) when you register with the tax agency (Modelo 036/037).

## What happens if you don't file them

Failing to file a mandatory modelo, or filing it late, leads to surcharges and possible penalties, even if the amount owed is zero. In other words, the obligation is to file the form, not just to pay when there's something due.

## In summary

- A modelo is an official form for declaring fiscal information to the tax agency.
- Not all of them involve a payment: some are self-assessments and others are informational summaries.
- Which modelos apply to you depends on your specific activity, they're not the same for every autónomo.
- Filing late (or not filing at all) has consequences even if you don't owe anything.
