Marta gives private classes in her pottery workshop. A student books four classes and pays for them in advance. Marta issues the invoice. A few days later, the student cancels all four and Marta refunds the money.

What does Marta do with that invoice?

::card
---

title: Short answer
background: white
shadow: true
---

- The original invoice is not annulled. The client paid, so the invoice was correct.
- Issue a **rectifying invoice (factura rectificativa) R1, by difference**, with the same amounts in negative.
- Refund the client.
- The original invoice and the rectifying invoice cancel each other out: the final result is zero.

::

## Why it is not annulled

Annulment is only for invoices that should never have been issued, for example a duplicate invoice or one for an operation that never existed.

Here the student really paid and Marta invoiced that payment. That is correct: when you charge in advance, VAT arises at the moment of payment, even if the class has not happened yet. The VAT Law puts it like this (art. 75.Dos):

> in taxable operations that give rise to advance payments before the taxable event takes place, the tax becomes chargeable when the price is collected, in full or in part, for the amounts actually received.

What happened afterwards, the cancellation, is corrected with a rectifying invoice.

## Which rectifying invoice to issue

**Reason R1.** It is the code for an operation that is undone after it was invoiced. The VAT Law covers it in article 80.Dos:

> When, by a final judicial or administrative decision, or in accordance with the law or commercial practice, taxable operations are left wholly or partly without effect [...] the taxable base will be changed by the corresponding amount.

It is not R4. R4 is for ordinary mistakes, and here there was no mistake.

**Type by difference (I).** The rectifying invoice shows only the change: the amounts of the original invoice with a minus sign.

It is also correct to do it **by substitution (S)**: the rectifying invoice shows what the invoice should be, 0 € if everything is cancelled, without writing negative amounts. Both ways lead to the same result, so it depends on what you prefer. We go through it with Tom's case in [Classes cancelled after invoicing: the rectifying invoice by substitution](/en/blog/cancelled-classes-substitution). The general differences between the two types are in [How to rectify an invoice](/en/blog/how-to-rectify-an-invoice).

## Which amounts

If the four classes cost 100 € plus VAT:

::card-group

::card
---

background: white
title: Original invoice
---

- Taxable base: 100 €
- VAT (21%): 21 €
- **Total: 121 €**

::

::card
---

background: white
shadow: true
title: Rectifying invoice
---

- Taxable base: -100 €
- VAT (21%): -21 €
- **Total: -121 €**

::

::

The rectifying invoice carries the same amounts as the original invoice, with a minus sign. Together they add up to zero: the client pays nothing and you pay no VAT for those classes.

If the student only cancels part of it (for example, they attended two classes and cancel the other two), it works the same way, but only for the classes you refund.

## In short

- If the client paid in advance, the invoice was correct. It is not annulled.
- When they cancel, you issue a rectifying invoice **R1, by difference**, with the same amounts in negative.
- You refund the money, and the final result is zero.
