Since 2023, self-employed workers in Spain (autónomos) no longer pay a flat Social Security contribution: they pay based on what they actually earn. The lower your net income, the lower your monthly quota; the higher it is, the more you pay. This "bracket" (tramo) system is what we break down here, with the figures in force for 2026.

## What is a contribution bracket

A bracket (tramo) is a range of monthly net income that corresponds to a contribution base (a minimum and a maximum) and, therefore, a specific monthly quota. When you register your activity, you choose a contribution base within your bracket's range; that base, multiplied by the contribution rate, determines what you pay each month.

For 2026, the total contribution rate is **30.50%**, broken down as:

- Common contingencies: 28.30%
- Temporary/permanent incapacity (IT/IP): 1.30%
- Intergenerational Equity Mechanism (MEI): 0.90% (up from 0.80% in 2025)

The absolute maximum contribution base is **€5,101.20/month**.

## How your net income is calculated

Net income is not the same as revenue. It's calculated as follows:

- **Sole trader (persona física):** (income − deductible expenses) × 0.93 (a generic 7% deduction applies).
- **Corporate autónomo (societario):** (income − deductible expenses) × 0.97 (a 3% deduction applies).

It's this estimated monthly net income that determines which bracket you fall into.

## What happens if your income varies during the year

Since you contribute based on a forecast of your net income, that estimate rarely matches reality exactly by year-end. That's why you can change your contribution base up to six times a year, and Social Security runs an **annual regularization**: if you earned more than forecast, they'll claim the difference; if you earned less, they'll refund what you overpaid.

## In summary

- You pay based on your actual net income, not a flat quota.
- There are 15 brackets in total: 3 in the reduced table and 12 in the general one.
- You can adjust your contribution base several times a year to stay closer to your real situation and avoid surprises at regularization time.

*Source: Orden PJC/297/2026, of 30 March (BOE 31 March 2026).*
